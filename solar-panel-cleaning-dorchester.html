Local conditions

Farmland, not seafront

What actually lands on a roof in Dorchester, and why it matters.

Agricultural dust, on an agricultural calendar

Dorchester is inland and surrounded by working farmland, so the soiling here is seasonal rather than constant. Ploughing in autumn and harvest in late summer both put a great deal of fine dry dust into the air, and it travels further than people expect on a still day.

Bigger roofs, and more of them

Barn conversions, farm buildings and the newer Poundbury housing all tend towards larger arrays than the town-centre average. Larger array, larger absolute loss for the same percentage — which is what makes maintenance pay here.

Much less salt

Being properly inland, Dorchester roofs escape the salt film that defines the coastal towns. That genuinely does mean a less aggressive cleaning regime is defensible.

Rural access

Long drives, gravel, farm gates and the occasional livestock field. Worth mentioning when you book so we arrive with the right kit and the right amount of time.

Our honest take for Dorchester: Inland sites are the ones where we are most likely to tell you that once a year is genuinely enough. We would rather say that than sell you visits you do not need.

What a visit covers

  • • A full clean of the array using deionised water, so nothing is left behind as it dries
  • • A documented inspection: mounting and clamps, cable runs and clips, isolators, and inverter readings
  • • Minor repairs on the spot where they are minor — a loose clip, a sagging cable, a slipped bird guard
  • • A written report each visit, so you have a history of the system rather than a vague memory
  • • A flag on anything that needs a qualified installer, with no attempt to do it ourselves

We are a maintenance and cleaning business, not an MCS-certified installer. If something needs an installer or an electrician, we will tell you plainly rather than have a go.
