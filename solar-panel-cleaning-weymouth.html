Local conditions

The most exposed coast we cover

What actually lands on a roof in Weymouth, and why it matters.

Directly onto the Channel, facing south

Weymouth takes weather off open sea with nothing between it and France. The prevailing south-westerly arrives carrying salt, and south-facing roofs — which is to say, the roofs with solar on them — catch it head on.

Portland stone dust

Quarrying on Portland puts fine limestone dust into the air, and it drifts. It is alkaline, pale, and it sits on glass rather than running off. Mixed with salt film it is noticeably harder to shift than ordinary road dust.

The gulls are not a joke here

Weymouth has one of the more determined urban gull populations on the south coast. Seafront and harbour-side roofs get fouled repeatedly through the breeding season, and the same roofs get it year after year.

Wind-driven debris

Exposed sites collect grit along the lower panel edges where the frame traps it. Left there it holds water, and in winter that is where moss starts.

Our honest take for Weymouth: Weymouth is the one place on our list where we would gently push back on an annual-only clean. The salt and gull load is simply higher here.

What a visit covers

  • • A full clean of the array using deionised water, so nothing is left behind as it dries
  • • A documented inspection: mounting and clamps, cable runs and clips, isolators, and inverter readings
  • • Minor repairs on the spot where they are minor — a loose clip, a sagging cable, a slipped bird guard
  • • A written report each visit, so you have a history of the system rather than a vague memory
  • • A flag on anything that needs a qualified installer, with no attempt to do it ourselves

We are a maintenance and cleaning business, not an MCS-certified installer. If something needs an installer or an electrician, we will tell you plainly rather than have a go.
